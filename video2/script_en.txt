# Video 2 (EN): Does one coffee a day make you a millionaire? The honest math of investing
# One line = one scene = one image. All numbers are example calculations at 7 percent return per year, no guarantee and no financial advice.
You have probably heard this one: skip your coffee, invest the money, and you will become a millionaire.
Sounds great, right? But is it actually true? Or is it just a nice myth from the internet?
In this video we do the honest math, with real numbers and no sugarcoating.
And by the end, you will understand what really works, and what leads you astray.
Phase one: the coffee math.
Let's say your coffee costs four euros. Every single day, for a year. That adds up to almost fourteen hundred and sixty euros.
That is about one hundred and twenty euros a month vanishing into your coffee cup.
Now imagine you invest exactly that amount every month instead of spending it.
We calculate with seven percent return per year. That is a commonly quoted average for broadly diversified stock ETFs over a very long time.
Important: this is not a guarantee. Some years the market drops hard, other years it climbs a lot.
Phase two: compound interest.
Albert Einstein supposedly called it the eighth wonder of the world. Whether that is true is unclear, but the idea behind it is solid.
Your money earns a return. And next year, that return earns a return of its own. This is called compound interest.
At the start it feels ridiculously slow. After a few years your portfolio looks barely different from a piggy bank.
But at some point it tips. Then your money works harder than you do.
Phase three: what you actually end up with.
After ten years, the coffee money has grown to almost twenty-one thousand euros. You paid in almost fifteen thousand.
After twenty years it is around sixty-two thousand euros, with about twenty-nine thousand paid in.
After thirty years it is roughly one hundred and forty-two thousand euros. That is already a lot of money.
And after forty years, you land at about three hundred thousand euros.
That is strong. But it is not a million yet.
Phase four: the uncomfortable truth.
So the line about coffee making you a millionaire is not quite right. It is more of a good start.
If you want a million in forty years, you need about four hundred euros a month at seven percent return.
At five percent return, it is already around six hundred and seventy-five euros a month.
And if you want to get there in thirty years, you need about eight hundred and fifty euros a month at seven percent.
So a million is not about coffee. It is about time and savings rate.
Phase five: and what about one hundred euros a month?
One hundred euros a month is realistic for many people. It is the number where most say: I can do that.
After thirty years at seven percent, that is almost one hundred and seventeen thousand euros. You paid in only thirty-six thousand.
After forty years it is about two hundred and forty-seven thousand euros, with forty-eight thousand paid in.
So the gain is many times what you put in yourself. That is the magic of time.
But it does not make you rich. Above all, it makes you free, flexible and independent.
Phase six: the biggest lever is not the return.
Many people hunt for the perfect stock or the best tip. But three things matter far more.
First: how early you start. If you start at nineteen, you have a ten-year head start on someone who starts at twenty-nine.
Second: how much you invest. If you raise your savings rate when your salary rises, it changes everything.
Third: how long you stay in. Anyone who sells at every crash almost always loses.
Phase seven: what gets in the way in real life.
First, inflation. Your money loses a bit of buying power every year. A million in forty years is worth less than a million today.
Second, taxes and costs. Gains are taxed, and funds have ongoing fees.
Third, swings. Your portfolio can drop on paper by thirty or forty percent for a while.
And the hardest part is not the math. It is keeping your nerves in those moments.
Phase eight: how to keep it simple.
Many beginners use a broadly diversified world ETF. It bundles thousands of companies into a single product.
Instead of betting on one winner, you buy the whole market. It is boring, but that is exactly the point.
A savings plan buys automatically every month, whether prices are high or low. You do not have to wait for the right moment.
Before that, though, you should have an emergency fund, so you do not have to sell your portfolio when something unexpected happens.
And if you have high-interest debt, paying it off is usually the safer return.
This was not financial advice, just a worked example. The decision is yours, ideally after your own research.
Phase nine: the honest answer.
Does one coffee a day make you a millionaire? No. Not on its own, not in twenty years, and not without patience.
But it can build you a six-figure fortune if you start early and stick with it.
And the best part: the million is often not the end goal, it is the journey that makes you financially stable.
