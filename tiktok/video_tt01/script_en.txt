# TikTok 1: Where your $5 coffee money actually goes
You pay five dollars for a coffee. Where does that money actually go? Let's follow it.
The beans, milk and the cup together usually cost well under a third of the price.
Roasting, shipping and the farmer's share are all packed into that small slice, which is why a single cup looks so cheap to make.
But a shop doesn't sell beans, it sells a seat, a warm room and ten minutes where nobody bothers you.
Most of the rest pays for the place: rent for a busy corner, often the biggest single cost.
Then comes the barista. Wages, taxes and training take another big slice of every cup you buy.
Add electricity, the machine, cleaning, and the card fee you paid just by tapping.
Whatever is left is the owner's profit, often only a few cents on the dollar.
Slow mornings make it worse, because rent and staff cost the same whether the line is long or empty.
That is why shops push the pastry next to the till: extra items cover the fixed costs far better than coffee alone.
So a coffee isn't expensive because of the coffee. You are paying for the corner, the people and the time.
Next time you pay five dollars, which part do you think costs the most?
