# TikTok 5: Why gas prices jump up fast and fall slowly
Oil gets cheaper, so why is the pump price still high a week later?
Economists even have a name for it: rockets and feathers.
Prices shoot up like a rocket, and drift down like a feather.
Crude oil is roughly half of what you pay for a gallon.
A ten dollar jump per barrel adds about twenty-five cents per gallon.
When oil rises, stations raise prices fast, because their thin margin disappears overnight.
When costs fall, nobody is in a hurry.
Studies find prices typically fall about twice as slowly as they rose.
One reason is you.
When prices climb, drivers hunt for the cheapest station, so stations must keep up.
When prices drop, most of us stop comparing, and stations enjoy fatter margins for a few weeks.
Nobody wants to cut first either, because rivals would just match the price.
After a couple of months it usually evens out, but those weeks add up.
So when oil drops, keep checking prices, because shopping around speeds up the feather.
Do you check gas prices before you fill up, or just pull in?
