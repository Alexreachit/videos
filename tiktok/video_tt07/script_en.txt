# TikTok 7: Why streaming prices keep rising
Your streaming bill just went up again, and it is not an accident.
In 2025, US prices for video subscriptions rose about seven times faster than overall inflation.
It starts with growth: about nine in ten US internet homes already pay for at least one service.
When almost everyone has signed up, new customers get hard to find.
So the easiest way to grow is to charge the people already paying a little more.
Meanwhile, the biggest services each spend well over ten billion dollars a year on shows and movies.
Then came cheaper plans with ads, which earn money twice: from you and from advertisers.
The average ad-free plan went from about nine dollars in 2020 to about sixteen in 2025.
Then the sharing crackdown: one big service estimated over a hundred million homes were using borrowed passwords.
Now those extra homes have to pay their own way, or the owner pays an extra fee.
About four in ten people cancel a service within six months, and many sign up again later.
Each price hike is a bet that most of you will grumble and stay.
