# TikTok 2: Why airlines sell more seats than exist
This plane has 180 seats, but the airline sold 190 tickets.
That's not a mistake. It's a calculated bet.
On a typical flight, roughly one in twenty passengers never shows up.
Business travelers change plans, connections run late, and some people simply oversleep.
An empty seat earns nothing, yet the plane burns almost the same fuel and needs the same crew.
So airlines study every route's no-show history and sell just enough extra tickets to cover the gaps.
Most days the math works: the extra passengers fill the empty seats, and nobody notices.
Fuller planes also split the flight's cost among more people, which helps keep fares lower.
But sometimes everyone shows up, and the gate agent has to start an auction.
Who will take a later flight for a 200 dollar voucher? No one? 300? 400?
The offer keeps climbing until someone says yes, because that is still cheaper than a plane full of angry passengers.
Getting bumped against your will is rare, typically just a few people in every 100,000.
So what price would make you give up your seat?
