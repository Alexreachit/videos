# TikTok 6: How your savings account makes the bank money
You put a thousand dollars into savings, and your bank starts making money on it.
A typical savings account in the US pays only about 0.4 percent a year.
On your thousand, that is roughly four dollars a year.
But your money does not sit in a vault with your name on it.
The bank keeps a slice ready for withdrawals and lends out much of the rest.
US rules have not even required banks to hold a minimum reserve since 2020.
Someone borrows it for a new car at roughly six percent a year.
Someone else carries a credit card balance at around twenty percent.
The gap between what you get and what borrowers pay is the bank's spread.
On average, US banks keep roughly three cents a year for every dollar they put to work.
That pays for branches, staff, apps and loans that never come back, and the rest is profit.
