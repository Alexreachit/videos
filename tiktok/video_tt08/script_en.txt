# TikTok 8: Why the warehouse-club hot dog never gets more expensive
A hot dog and a soda for just $1.50.
At Costco, that price has not changed since 1985.
Adjusted for inflation, it would cost more than four dollars today.
Meat, buns, wages and rent all got pricier, so each combo probably earns close to nothing.
Yet the chain sells roughly 200 million of them every year.
That is called a loss leader, a deal so good that it pulls you through the door.
And once you are inside, you rarely leave with just a hot dog.
But here is the real trick: you had to pay just to walk in.
A basic membership costs about $65 a year.
Around 80 million households pay that fee.
And roughly nine out of ten of them renew.
Together, those fees bring in about $5 billion a year.
That is equal to roughly two thirds of the company's entire yearly profit.
So the shelves can run on thin margins, and the hot dog becomes pure advertising.
A co-founder reportedly warned his successor that if he ever raised the hot dog price, he would kill him.
The hot dog is not the product, your membership is.
Would you pay a yearly fee just to shop cheaper?
