# TikTok 10: Why a dollar buys less every year
Twenty years ago, a hundred dollars filled this cart.
Today, the same cart costs roughly one hundred seventy dollars.
That slow, steady rise in prices is called inflation.
Statisticians track it with a basket of thousands of everyday prices, from rent and food to fuel and haircuts.
Prices usually climb for three simple reasons.
First, when people want to buy more than shops can supply, sellers can raise prices.
Second, when energy, materials or wages get more expensive, businesses pass part of that on.
Third, when money and credit grow faster than the goods they chase, each dollar is worth a little less.
That's why many central banks, like the Fed and the European Central Bank, aim for about two percent a year.
Small and steady, so people can plan, save and borrow without nasty surprises.
But two percent adds up: after ten years, prices are about twenty percent higher.
After twenty years, the same dollar buys roughly a third less.
That's why cash in a drawer quietly loses value, even though the number on the bill never changes.
