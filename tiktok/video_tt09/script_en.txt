# TikTok 9: How "Buy now, pay later" really makes money
Four easy payments of $25, and zero interest.
So who is paying for that loan?
It splits your purchase into four chunks, paid over about six weeks.
You pay no interest, so the lender needs another way to earn money.
Mostly, it is the store that pays.
On every sale, the shop hands the lender a fee, typically between 2 and 8 percent.
A regular card payment often costs only around 2 to 3 percent.
So why would a store pay more?
Because a $100 jacket suddenly feels like a $25 jacket.
Shoppers check out more, abandon fewer carts and fill bigger baskets.
The store gives up a few dollars to win a bigger sale.
Then come the late fees.
Miss a payment, and many lenders charge a flat fee of several dollars.
In one US government study, about one in eight borrowers paid a late fee.
Still, store fees earned lenders roughly nine times more than late fees.
Some also earn interest on longer plans and money from ads in their apps.
So the free loan is mostly paid for by the shop, and sometimes by you.
