# Video 2 (new): From the first euro to the first million: your money at every level
# One line = one scene = one image. Worked example with 7 percent return per year, no guarantee, no financial advice.
Imagine you are nineteen, your account is empty, and someone tells you that in forty years you will have a million.
That sounds like a fairy tale. So today I will show you every single step on the way, with honest numbers.
There are seven levels. From zero euros to the first million. And at one of them almost everyone quits.
All calculations assume seven percent return per year. That is not a guarantee and not financial advice, just a worked example.
Phase one: level zero, the coffee cup.
You buy a coffee for four euros every day. You barely notice it.
But over a year that is almost fourteen hundred and sixty euros. About one hundred and twenty a month.
One hundred and twenty euros. That is a phone plan, a few dinners out, a bit of shopping.
And now the question: what happens if you invest exactly that amount every month instead of spending it?
Phase two: level one, the first thousand euros.
The first month feels great. One hundred and twenty euros land in your account. You are proud.
After nine months it is about one thousand euros. That is nothing that changes your life yet.
And here comes the first trap: the people around you buy new shoes, a vacation, a new phone.
You see none of it in your account. This is where many people give up. They think it is not worth it.
But whoever keeps going now collects something money cannot buy: a habit.
Phase three: level two, the first ten thousand euros.
After about six years you reach ten thousand euros. You only paid in about nine thousand of it.
The difference is about a thousand euros that your money earned all by itself. Compound interest shows up for the first time.
And then comes the first crash. Within weeks your account falls by thirty percent.
Your gut screams: sell! And that is exactly the moment where investors really lose money.
Whoever holds usually stands on top again after a few years. Whoever sells locks the loss in forever.
Phase four: level three, fifty thousand euros.
After about eighteen years you are at around fifty thousand euros. You have money you put in, and money that works for you.
And it gets interesting: in some years your account earns more than you pay in.
This is the turning point. From here on, time does more than discipline.
But temptations wait here too: a new car, a bigger vacation, a loan for things you do not need.
The trick is not giving up everything, but deciding on purpose what really matters to you.
Phase five: level four, the first hundred thousand euros.
After a good twenty-five years you crack the hundred thousand. Many say the first hundred thousand is the hardest.
From now on your account grows by several thousand euros a year without you paying in a single extra cent.
You notice: your money has started working for you. Not just you working for your money.
And still this is not freedom yet. It is a cushion, not a retirement from working life.
Phase six: level five, three hundred thousand euros.
If you keep going for a full forty years, your account stands at around three hundred thousand euros. From one hundred and twenty euros a month.
Of that you only paid in about sixty thousand yourself. The rest is time and compound interest.
That is strong. But it is not a million yet. And someone should tell you that honestly.
The line that a coffee makes you a millionaire is a nice start, but only half the truth.
Phase seven: level six, the million.
If you want a million in forty years, you need around four hundred euros a month at seven percent.
At five percent return it is already around six hundred and seventy-five euros a month.
And whoever wants the million in thirty years needs around eight hundred and fifty euros at seven percent.
So the million is not a question of coffee. It is a question of time and savings rate.
Phase eight: the biggest lever.
First: how early you start. Whoever starts at nineteen has a ten-year head start on someone who starts at twenty-nine.
Second: how much you invest. Whoever raises the savings rate when the salary rises changes everything.
Third: how long you hold on. Whoever sells in every crash almost always loses.
And then there is something nobody tells you: money is not the goal. It is the tool.
It buys you time, choices and peace. That is what most people are really looking for.
To sum up: one hundred and twenty euros a month is no road to luxury, but a very solid road to freedom.
If you start investing even a part of it from today, you are already further than most.
